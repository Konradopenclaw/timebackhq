Start with the work that keeps pulling the owner back in.

Most businesses do not lose time in one dramatic failure. They lose it in repeated friction that becomes normal: missed follow-up, unclear handoffs, scattered notes, late decisions, and software that never became a trusted operating rhythm. The first job is to make the next action easier to see and own.

  • Follow-up spread across inboxes, texts, calendars, and memory
  • Customer updates that depend on whoever remembered last
  • Project details scattered across tools and private notes
  • Estimates, invoices, or next actions sent later than intended
  • Routine decisions that still require the owner to reconstruct the context
